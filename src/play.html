The idea

What happens when you stop trying to get it right?

Most of us learn to think of creativity as a talent. Something some people have, and others don't.

Play Inquiry approaches it differently.

Guided prompts, simple materials and playful experiments give people a starting point, then leave room for them to discover where it goes.

There is no single correct result. The point is to try, explore and see what happens.

About Oselu

A practice by Oselu Irabor

I'm a visual artist, illustrator and designer interested in how we see, make and communicate ideas.

Play Inquiry grows out of my wider creative practice and my interest in making creative participation feel accessible. Through guided drawing, prompts and hands-on experiments, I'm exploring what happens when people are given room to try things without the pressure to get everything right.

The workshops are an extension of that curiosity: a way to make, learn and discover alongside other people.

From first lines to oil pastel: the Lagos session

A Play Inquiry Session for individuals, held in collaboration with Ayanfe Haven. Twelve participants came into drawing through simple exercises, guided prompts and a progression from pencil sketches to oil-pastel paintings.

On this page

The brief

Introduce a group of twelve people to drawing and help each of them develop their own work, starting with simple exercises rather than an intimidating blank page.

The purpose was to create an approachable, hands-on experience for people with different levels of familiarity with drawing.

The question

How do you help people begin making something when uncertainty or the fear of getting it wrong might otherwise stop them?

The response was to build a sequence of approachable activities, giving participants a starting point before asking them to develop their own work.

The approach

Instead of starting with a blank page and a big subject, the session moved in stages, each one building on the last.

  1. 1

    Warm-up sketching

    Simple, low-pressure exercises to loosen the hand and get past the fear of the first mark.

  2. 2

    Easing in

    Building from those exercises towards looking closely and drawing a real subject.

  3. 3

    Drawing

    Each participant developed an individual drawing.

  4. 4

    Painting with oil pastel

    Colour was added through layering and blending to finish the piece.

What participants made

Participants left with their own drawings and oil-pastel paintings. The subjects were simple still lifes: bottles, cups, fruit and flowers.

What comes next

The Lagos session was a starting point for a broader workshop practice. I'm developing the format into repeatable creative experiences for individuals, communities and teams, using drawing and hands-on experimentation to make room for curiosity and discovery.

The aim is to keep testing the approach, refining the facilitation and exploring collaborations with people and organisations interested in creative participation.

Play Inquiry Sessions

01

Co-host a session

An artist, venue, brand or other creative collaborator co-hosts a Play Inquiry Session for their audience. Play Inquiry develops and facilitates the session.

The core experience remains the same, while the host, audience and setting can change. Commercial and operational arrangements are agreed in advance.

02

Commission a team workshop

A company or organisation commissions a workshop for its team, offsite or gathering.

Each session is adapted to the team's context, objectives, group size and available time. Prior drawing experience is not required.

Plan a team workshop →

In the room

A designed experience, not an art class brought into the office

Every workshop is built around guided prompts, playful experimentation and hands-on making, so everyone has a way in, whatever their experience. What it offers is the experience itself: time to try, make and talk about it together.

  • People try things
  • They make things, alone and together
  • They exchange ideas
  • They interpret the same prompt in different ways
  • They reflect on the process

Planning a session? It helps to share

  • Who the session is for, and roughly how many people
  • Where it could happen, and how much time you have
  • Any theme, occasion or question you'd like it to explore
  • Rough timing. Nothing needs to be fixed yet
